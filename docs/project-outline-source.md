# **1. Background**

Organizations across industries are experiencing unprecedented growth in
data volume, variety, and velocity due to rapid digital transformation
initiatives. As enterprises increasingly adopt cloud-native
technologies, traditional data warehousing environments and legacy ETL
(Extract, Transform, Load) systems have become significant barriers to
agility, scalability, and innovation. These legacy systems often consist
of complex ETL workflows developed over many years using proprietary
tools, platform-specific scripting languages, and tightly coupled
architectures. While these systems continue to support critical business
operations, they introduce substantial technical debt, making
modernization projects time-consuming, expensive, and
resource-intensive.

The evolution of cloud computing has fundamentally transformed the way
organizations manage enterprise data. Cloud-native platforms such as
Snowflake provide scalable storage, elastic compute resources, improved
security, simplified infrastructure management, and consumption-based
pricing models. These capabilities enable organizations to build modern
analytical ecosystems capable of supporting advanced analytics,
artificial intelligence, and real-time decision-making. Consequently,
enterprises are increasingly investing in large-scale migration programs
to modernize legacy data warehouses and ETL pipelines.

Despite the availability of advanced cloud platforms, the modernization
journey remains a significant challenge. Legacy ETL environments
typically contain thousands of transformation rules, stored procedures,
workflow dependencies, scheduling configurations, and business logic
accumulated over many years of development. Much of this knowledge
exists only within legacy codebases or in the experience of senior
developers. Documentation is often incomplete, outdated, or unavailable,
making migration efforts heavily dependent on manual analysis by subject
matter experts. This increases project duration, introduces operational
risks, and significantly raises migration costs.

Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) has emerged as a
transformative technology capable of understanding, generating,
summarizing, and transforming software artifacts. Large Language Models
(LLMs) can analyze complex source code, generate documentation,
recommend optimized implementations, and assist software engineers
during modernization initiatives. While significant research has
explored the application of Generative AI in software development, its
structured application to enterprise ETL modernization remains an
emerging field with considerable research potential.

This project proposes a Generative AI-Assisted Framework for Modernizing
Legacy ETL and Data Warehouse Systems to Cloud-Native Data Platforms
using Snowflake. Rather than focusing solely on automated code
conversion, the proposed framework aims to support the entire
modernization lifecycle. The framework will incorporate AI-assisted
legacy system analysis, metadata interpretation, dependency
identification, transformation rule extraction, migration
recommendations, documentation generation, validation support, and
governance considerations.

Snowflake has been selected as the target cloud-native platform because
of its architecture, scalability, separation of storage and compute,
integrated data-sharing capabilities, and growing adoption across global
enterprises. The framework will investigate how Generative AI can
complement Snowflake-based modernization initiatives by improving
productivity, reducing manual effort, enhancing documentation quality,
and supporting consistent migration practices.

From a business perspective, successful modernization initiatives
directly influence operational efficiency, time-to-market,
infrastructure costs, data governance, and organizational
competitiveness. Therefore, this project combines technical innovation
with strategic management concepts learned during the HACKATHON program,
including digital transformation, technology management, project
management, innovation management, operations strategy, and business
value realization.

The proposed research intends to develop a structured modernization
framework that can serve as a practical reference for organizations
undertaking legacy ETL migration projects. By integrating Generative AI
capabilities with established cloud migration methodologies, the project
aims to contribute both to industry practice and academic knowledge.

# **2. Requirement Statement / Problem Statement**

Many organizations continue to rely on legacy ETL and enterprise data
warehouse systems that have evolved over decades. Although these systems
are functionally stable, they suffer from limited scalability, high
maintenance costs, increasing technical debt, and dependence on
specialized development skills. As organizations accelerate cloud
adoption strategies, migration to cloud-native platforms has become a
strategic priority. However, existing modernization approaches remain
largely manual and require significant effort to analyze legacy code,
identify business rules, document workflows, redesign architectures, and
validate migrated solutions.

Several practical challenges are commonly encountered during enterprise
modernization projects:

-   Inadequate or outdated technical documentation.

-   Complex dependencies between ETL jobs and workflows.

-   Limited understanding of embedded business rules.

-   High manual effort during code analysis and transformation.

-   Difficulty validating migrated implementations.

-   Increased project cost and extended delivery timelines.

-   Knowledge dependency on experienced developers.

-   Inconsistent migration practices across teams.

Although Generative AI has demonstrated promising capabilities in code
generation and software assistance, its application in enterprise ETL
modernization is still evolving. There is a need for a structured
framework that integrates AI capabilities into different stages of the
modernization lifecycle while maintaining governance, validation,
transparency, and business alignment.

Accordingly, the central problem addressed by this project is:

***How can a Generative AI-assisted framework improve the efficiency,
consistency, documentation quality, and governance of legacy ETL
modernization projects targeting Snowflake-based cloud-native data
platforms?***

The project seeks to answer this question through a systematic framework
that combines AI-assisted analysis, migration guidance, documentation
generation, and validation mechanisms.

# **6. Proposed Methodology**

This project adopts a structured research methodology consisting of
multiple sequential phases. The methodology combines academic research
with practical industry practices to develop an AI-assisted
modernization framework.

## **Phase 1: Literature Review**

A comprehensive literature review will be conducted to study:

-   Legacy ETL modernization

-   Cloud migration strategies

-   Enterprise Data Warehousing

-   Snowflake Architecture

-   Metadata-driven engineering

-   Generative AI

-   Large Language Models

-   Software modernization research

The review will establish the theoretical foundation of the proposed
framework.

## **Phase 2: Requirement Analysis**

This phase identifies common enterprise modernization challenges by
reviewing:

-   Legacy ETL environments

-   Existing migration methodologies

-   Business requirements

-   Technical constraints

-   Governance requirements

## **Phase 3: Framework Design**

Based on the identified challenges, an AI-assisted modernization
framework will be designed. The framework consists of the following
logical components:

**Input Layer:** Legacy ETL Jobs, SQL Scripts, Metadata, Mapping
Documents, Workflow Definitions

**AI Analysis Layer:** Code Understanding, Dependency Discovery,
Business Rule Extraction, Documentation Generation, Migration
Recommendation

**Transformation Layer:** ELT Conversion, SQL Optimization, dbt Model
Suggestions, Snowflake Object Mapping

**Validation Layer:** Business Rule Verification, Metadata Validation,
Quality Checks, Documentation Review

**Output Layer:** Modernized Design, Migration Documentation, AI
Recommendations, Governance Reports

Each layer feeds sequentially into the next, forming an end-to-end
AI-assisted modernization pipeline from legacy input through to
governed, documented output.

## **Phase 4: Proof-of-Concept Development**

A conceptual prototype will demonstrate selected capabilities of the
framework, such as:

-   AI-assisted SQL explanation

-   ETL workflow documentation

-   Transformation recommendation

-   Metadata interpretation

-   Migration planning

The proof-of-concept is intended to illustrate feasibility rather than
deliver a production-ready solution.

## **Phase 5: Framework Evaluation**

The framework will be evaluated against qualitative criteria including:

-   Ease of adoption

-   Documentation completeness

-   Governance support

-   Maintainability

-   Migration consistency

-   Expected productivity improvements

Feedback from industry practices and published research will be
considered during the evaluation.

## **Research Methodology Flow**

*Literature Review → Requirement Analysis → Legacy ETL Assessment →
Generative AI Analysis → Framework Design → Snowflake Architecture
Mapping → Proof of Concept → Validation → Business Benefit Evaluation →
Project Documentation*

# **7. Plan of Work**

The successful execution of this project requires a structured and
phased approach to ensure that all research objectives are achieved
within the stipulated project timeline. The work plan has been designed
to balance academic research, technical exploration, framework
development, validation, and documentation activities. Since this
project combines concepts from Data Engineering, Artificial
Intelligence, Cloud Computing, and Business Management, each phase
contributes incrementally toward the final deliverables.

The project will commence with an extensive review of literature related
to legacy ETL modernization, cloud-native data platforms, enterprise
data warehousing, and Generative Artificial Intelligence. This phase
will establish the theoretical foundation of the research and help
identify existing methodologies, research gaps, and opportunities for
innovation.

The second phase will focus on understanding enterprise modernization
challenges through analysis of legacy ETL systems, migration practices,
Snowflake architecture, and AI-assisted software engineering techniques.
During this phase, functional and non-functional requirements for the
proposed framework will be identified.

The third phase involves the design of the proposed Generative
AI-Assisted Modernization Framework. The framework will define the
interaction between legacy ETL assets, metadata repositories,
AI-assisted analysis modules, migration planning, Snowflake
architecture, validation mechanisms, and governance components.

The fourth phase will develop a conceptual proof-of-concept
demonstrating selected capabilities of the proposed framework. The
proof-of-concept will illustrate how Generative AI can assist in legacy
code understanding, workflow summarization, documentation generation,
migration planning, and transformation recommendations.

The fifth phase will evaluate the proposed framework using qualitative
performance indicators including usability, scalability, governance
support, documentation quality, migration consistency, and expected
productivity improvements. Feedback from published research and industry
practices will be incorporated to strengthen the framework.

The final phase will focus on report preparation, refinement of the
proposed framework, incorporation of supervisor feedback, preparation of
presentation material, and completion of all academic deliverables
required by Hackathon.
