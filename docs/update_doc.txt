1) Problem & Customer Focus

Enterprises across banking, healthcare, insurance, retail, and telecom are accelerating cloud adoption, but legacy ETL/data warehouse systems stand in the way — carrying years of undocumented business rules and dependencies known only to a handful of senior engineers. The real pain isn't the migration itself; it's everything that has to happen before migration can safely begin — reverse-engineering what the old system actually does. A representative legacy script built for this project makes this concrete: it contained two business rules (a 90-day order discount and a 24-month order cutoff) with zero documented rationale anywhere in the code. This is the exact tribal-knowledge problem that turns a routine migration into a multi-week investigation, and what the framework targets directly. (Maps to Objective 1 in the requirement document.)

3) AI Solution & Innovation

A layered framework — Input, AI Analysis, Transformation, Human Review Checkpoint, Validation, Migration Planning, and Output — moves legacy code through AI-assisted extraction to a Snowflake/dbt-ready design, with every AI output checked before being treated as final. A working proof-of-concept was built and run end-to-end: it correctly identified every business rule in the sample, generated a Snowflake-ready transformation, and correctly flagged the two undocumented rules for human review instead of guessing at them, with zero fabricated table or column references. It was also tested against a second, multi-step workflow sample to confirm it holds up beyond a single script. The innovation is treating human review and fact-checking as core pipeline stages, not an afterthought bolted onto code generation. (Maps to Objectives 3 and 4.)

4) Standing Out from Competitors

Existing tools like Snowflake SnowConvert, AWS Schema Conversion Tool, and BladeBridge/Datometry are strong at syntax-level code conversion but weak at explaining why a business rule exists or producing documentation a business reviewer can use. Generic AI copilots (ChatGPT-style) offer flexible code explanation but have no structured verification step, so nothing stops them from confidently inventing a rule that isn't actually in the code. This framework is documentation-first (business rules are the primary deliverable, not a side effect of conversion), fact-checks every AI claim against the source before a human sees it, is human-in-the-loop by design rather than autonomous, and isn't locked to a single AI vendor. (Not a named Objective in the requirement document — added specifically for this submission's evaluation criteria.)

5) Revenue & Business Growth

This is the one area that remains a genuine open item rather than a filled-in answer: the requirement document's Objective 5 asks for a qualitative business-benefit evaluation (productivity, governance, documentation quality), not a revenue or commercialization case, and no work has been done yet to define pricing, a commercial model, or a quantified ROI case. The only grounded anchor available is that the original project brief names banking, healthcare, insurance, retail, and telecom as the industries EXL already serves and is investing in — a reasonable starting point for buyer conversations, but not market research. Target buyer segments, a commercial model (internal accelerator vs. client-facing offering vs. licensed tool), and a scalability path from one sample script to a real client's full legacy estate all still need dedicated work. This is flagged honestly rather than estimated in the abstract.


The natural target segment is retail and D2C brands modernizing legacy ETL to Snowflake, where the highest-value entry point is de-risking pricing, discount, and customer-tier logic during migration — not customer analytics or marketing efficiency, which is a downstream outcome this framework doesn't directly produce. Two commercialization paths are worth testing: a diagnostic/audit-style offering that runs the existing pipeline against a prospective client's real legacy pipeline to surface undocumented risk before a full engagement is sold (needs no new build), and a longer-term tiered-SaaS accelerator for repeat use across similar clients (premature today, since it assumes a repeatable multi-tenant product that hasn't been built yet). Both are untested ideas, not validated business models — the next real client engagement is the opportunity to find out which one actually lands.
